# Self-Custody & Sovereignty Signal Report — Week of Oct 20 – 26, 2026

## Top 3 Signals

### 1. Eclair 0.14.4 and 0.14.5 Are Security Releases; 0.14.5 Fixes Fund-Theft Flaws in Trampoline Routing and Taproot Splicing (Optech #428)
🔴 Strong · in-force · technical · threat, key-mgmt · [Protocol blog]

**Summary:** Bitcoin Optech #428 covers Eclair 0.14.4 and 0.14.5 as security releases. In 0.14.5, Eclair #3409 fixes a trampoline-payment flaw that could let a malicious payer steal funds from a forwarding node, and Eclair #3411 fixes a flaw that could let a malicious channel peer make Eclair sign an invalid splice and steal funds. Optech attributes the Origin-header rejection (a defence against cross-site request forgery on the API) to 0.14.4, and says the project strongly recommends upgrading.
**Filter:** People who run their own Lightning node hold their channel funds, and these flaws let a counterparty take them from a node that has not upgraded.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/23/ · 2026-10-23

### 2. Safenet Launches: Four Security Firms Assess Safe Pro Transactions Before Execution, Replacing the Interface Warning
🔴 Strong · in-force · technical · ux, threat, agentic · [Protocol blog] · `third-party-verdict-attestation`

**Summary:** The post says four Sentinel firms (BlockSec, Candide, IntentGuard and OpenCover) assess a Safe Pro transaction before it executes, on Ethereum mainnet, Arbitrum and Gnosis Chain. Each firm bonds its verdict and incorrect verdicts can be slashed, split verdicts go to arbitration, and validators combine the verdicts into one onchain attestation visible in the Safenet explorer. It says Safenet replaces the interface-level warning with an independently verified verdict. Checks become visible to all Safe multisig users in 2027, with an optional Safenet Guard that executes a transaction only with a valid attestation for that exact transaction, plus a delayed escape hatch.
**Filter:** The pre-signing check a multisig user relies on is performed by third parties and shown as an attestation, and the optional Guard would make that attestation a condition of execution.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://safe.global/blog/safenet-launches · 2026-10-21

### 3. Lichen: A Single-Issuer Layer 2 Whose Users Can Redeem Without the Hub's Cooperation (Optech #428)
🟡 Weak · early · technical · recovery, key-mgmt · [Protocol blog]

**Summary:** Optech reports that Adam Gibson posted Lichen to the Bitcoin-Dev mailing list with a paper and proof-of-concept code. Each user holds a 2-of-2 contract with the hub; if the hub refuses or does not respond, the user generates a Groth16 proof that the sequencer signed a state recording the returned tokens, and disputes are settled onchain through presigned BitVMX transactions. Gibson acknowledges the sequencer can still cost the user or the hub funds.
**Filter:** Concerns whether a user of an issuer-run layer 2 can get back to onchain bitcoin with their own signatures when the issuer does not cooperate.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/23/ · 2026-10-23

## On the radar
- **Proposal: Private Descriptor Syntax That Treats Hash Preimages Like Keys (BIP379 Extension, Optech #428)** · 🟡 Weak · technical · key-mgmt: João Dias posted to Delving Bitcoin a proposal to extend BIP379 so a wallet can import a hash preimage from a descriptor and sign directly, for example `sha256(preimage(HEX))`, arguing a preimage "should be treated more like a key than a signature". It covers only preimages known at descriptor creation, and leaves open whether to amend BIP379 or write a new BIP and whether hardware wallets should handle preimage secrets. [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/23/ · 2026-10-23
- **Balsamic: One-round Threshold UOV from GPU-Accelerated FHE** · 🟡 Weak · academic · key-mgmt: The paper presents a homomorphic Unbalanced Oil and Vinegar (UOV) signer and its use for one-round threshold UOV signing, reporting 10.7 s per online partial signing on an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada and 7.77 s on an NVIDIA GB300 in 2-out-of-3 semihonest signing, with 5.25 MB per response. [IACR paper] https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2604 · 2026-10-22
- **Tritone: Pairwise Garbled Disputes for Multi-Party Proof Verification on Bitcoin** · 🟡 Weak · academic · key-mgmt: The paper presents a protocol that lets Bitcoin enforce the outcome of verifying a Groth16 proof without consensus changes, with funds secure as long as one of N fixed operators is honest, using Bitcoin bridges as the showcase. [IACR paper] https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2612 · 2026-10-23
