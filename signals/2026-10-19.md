# Self-Custody & Sovereignty Signal Report — Week of Oct 13 – 19, 2026

## Top 3 Signals

### 1. Ledger Agent Intent: One Approval Queue for AI-Agent Requests, Approved on the Hardware Signer
🔴 Strong · in-force · technical · agentic, hardware, ux · [Protocol blog]

**Summary:** Requests from a user's AI agents wait in one queue and are approved asynchronously on the signer's Secure Screen, with Transaction Check and Clear Signing. The options are Approve once, Always allow and Deny, and nothing is signed until the user confirms on the device. Within a budget the user defines, an agent can make micro-payments without asking each time, and budget top-up requests are approved in the same queue. The post says it launched at Ledger Op3n 2026 and works on the signer the user already owns.
**Filter:** Delegated agent authority becomes a standing-rule decision ("Always allow", budgets) confirmed on a hardware device rather than a per-transaction prompt.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://www.ledger.com/blog-introducing-ledger-agent-intent · 2026-10-15

### 2. Update: Ledger Responds to Hardware Implant Attack on Devices Returned Through Resellers
🟠 Emerging · formal · technical · hardware, threat · [Vendor security report]

**Summary:** Ledger says a criminal group bought genuine devices, opened them, installed hidden hardware meant to capture recovery phrases during setup, and returned them to distribution through resellers in Southeast Asia. Ledger says there was no flaw in its chip, operating system or servers. Its Donjon lab dissected the implant, units examined from retail and warehouse samples were clean, and it asked Authorized Resellers and Distributors to suspend sales in Southeast Asia and Hong Kong. The post gives no loss totals, affected models or steps for affected users, and a fuller technical disclosure is to follow.
**Filter:** Custody of a device's seed generation depends on the sales channel it passed through before the user opened it, and the maker says the secure element was not the failure point.
**Source:** [Vendor security report] https://www.ledger.com/blog-response-hardware-implant-attack · 2026-10-15

### 3. Eclair Fixes Two Memory-Exhaustion DoS Bugs in v0.14.1 (Optech #427)
🟠 Emerging · formal · technical · threat, key-mgmt · [Protocol blog]

**Summary:** Two denial-of-service bugs affect Eclair v0.14.0 and earlier and are fixed in v0.14.1 (Eclair #3324, released in July). One is a race in the duplicate temporary_channel_id check that leaked about 25 kB per repeated open_channel; the other bypasses the pending-channel limit. In one test a single connection exhausted 4 GB of memory in about 48 minutes, and the node then crashed on every restart. The newsletter says mobile wallets using an Eclair LSP, such as Phoenix, are unaffected.
**Filter:** People who run their own Lightning node carry this exposure themselves, and the remedy is to upgrade.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/16/ · 2026-10-16

## UX & Interaction Signals

### Ledger Atom Preview: Secure Mode Takes Over the Screen for Approvals, with a Secure Bar
🟡 Weak · early · technical · ux, hardware · [Protocol blog] · `trusted-display-mode-indicator`

**What changed:** Ledger's architecture post for Ledger Atom says that when an action needs approval the device switches to Secure Mode, the Ledger OS takes over the screen, and a Secure Bar lights up to show which environment is active. It states "What you see is exactly what you approve" and says a hardware switch cuts camera and microphone. Atom is in development, with delivery estimated for Q2 2027 ("an estimate, not a commitment") and reservations open.
**Experience filter:** The user would have to read the Secure Bar to know whether the approval prompt comes from the trusted environment; the post does not describe the confirmation flow.
**Design read (analysis):** A persistent hardware indicator for the trusted display is one candidate answer to "who is asking me to approve this", but the source gives too little flow detail to judge it.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://www.ledger.com/blog-ledger-atom-security-architecture · 2026-10-15
