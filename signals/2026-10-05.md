# Self-Custody & Sovereignty Signal Report — Week of Sep 28–Oct 5, 2026

## Top 3 Signals

### 1. SEC Proposal Would Address How Investment Advisers and Funds Can Custody Crypto Assets
🔴 Strong · regulatory · regulation, sovereign-adoption · [Regulatory filing]

**Summary:** The SEC proposed the first explicit regulatory framework for crypto asset custody by registered investment advisers and regulated funds (Release IA-7023). Notably, the proposal permits self-custody under enumerated conditions and expands the custodian pool to state trust companies. Commissioner Peirce's concurrent statement explicitly defends investors' right to true self-custody and warns against rules that "force investors to custody their assets with someone else" — framing the internal debate as a rights question, not a compliance one.

**Filter:** Changes WHO holds the keys at the institutional level. The rule draws the line between permitted custodians (third-party, state trust company, or self-custody under specified conditions) and determines whether institutional sovereign control remains with the asset holder or is mandated by regulatory intermediation.

**Source:** [Regulatory filing] https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-100-sec-proposal-would-address-how-investment-advisers-funds-can-custody-crypto-assets-under-federal · Oct 1, 2026 · 60-day public comment period opens upon Federal Register publication.

### 2. Bitcoin Core PSBT SIGHASH_SINGLE Signature Reuse Vulnerability (PR #35984)
🔴 Strong · technical · threat, key-mgmt · [Protocol blog]

**Summary:** A flaw in Bitcoin Core's PSBT signing logic allows a `SIGHASH_SINGLE` signature created without a corresponding output to commit to a constant hash value that an attacker can replay to spend other UTXOs controlled by the same private key. Fix is tracked in PR #35984.

**Filter:** Changes HOW sovereignty is exercised. Self-custodians using Bitcoin Core's built-in wallet for PSBT signing are exposed: a malformed signature ceremony can leave UTXOs under the same key permanently at risk of unauthorized spend.

**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/02/ · Oct 2, 2026 (Bitcoin Optech Newsletter #425)

---

### 3. Bitcoin Core Wallet Encryption Silent Failure Modes (PR #35752)
🔴 Strong · technical · threat, key-mgmt · [Protocol blog]

**Summary:** Two silent failure modes in Bitcoin Core's wallet encryption were identified: a failed database write can cause a passphrase change to appear successful while the old passphrase remains active post-reload; separately, the encryption routine can return a success code while private keys remain in plaintext in the wallet database. Both conditions leave keys exposed without user awareness.

**Filter:** Changes HOW sovereignty is exercised. Self-custodians relying on Bitcoin Core's native encryption for key protection at rest may believe their keys are secured after receiving a success confirmation — without knowing they are not. The user's sovereignty assumption is false.

**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/02/ · Oct 2, 2026 (Bitcoin Optech Newsletter #425)

🟠 Emerging · technical · ux, identity · [Protocol blog] · **ux_pattern:** binary security/privacy tradeoff gate at onboarding (non-granular, authentication-tier-gated)

**Summary:** MetaMask's "Basic Functionality" setting is a single all-or-nothing switch that simultaneously controls transaction simulation, phishing detection, and security alerts; users cannot disable individual features. **Experience filter:** Users must choose at onboarding — via "Manage Default Settings" — whether to trade all protective simulation and alert layers for reduced data sharing. OAuth-authenticated users have no such choice and cannot replicate the privacy-first setup regardless of preference.
