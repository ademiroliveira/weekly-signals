# Self-Custody & Sovereignty Signal Report — Week of Oct 27 – Nov 2, 2026

## Top 3 Signals

### 1. Binance Drained of About $6.3B Across 10 Chains; Galaxy Reports an EIP-7702 Sweep of a Hot Wallet and North Korea Attribution
🟠 Emerging · formal · institutional · threat, key-mgmt · [Institutional report]

**Summary:** Galaxy says Binance was drained of roughly $6.3b across 10 blockchains, and that the attacker first used EIP-7702 on Ethereum to turn a Binance hot wallet into a sweeper contract before emptying nine more chains within minutes. Galaxy relays Binance's preliminary finding that a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop gave access to a service container, with "no evidence of any wallet private key leak", and quotes Co-CEO Richard Teng saying Binance will cover all losses from the SAFU fund and company funds, while noting the SAFU fund (about $1b in BTC) does not come close to covering the loss. Galaxy says TRM Labs and Chainalysis attribute the attack to TraderTraitor, a North Korean unit, and that Tether, Circle, Ripple and World Liberty froze roughly $2.5–3bn between them. Chainalysis puts the unauthorized transfers at approximately $6.4 billion and the frozen total at about $2.8 billion, and says its strongest evidence for the attribution was Binance funds commingling on-chain with funds from the Bitget theft. Galaxy says withdrawals had not been re-enabled as of writing.
**Filter:** Funds that users leave with a custodian sit behind the custodian's operational wallets, and in this account a hot wallet's authority was redirected, with stablecoin issuer freezes the recovery the pages report.
**Source:** [Institutional report] https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/here-we-go-again-binance-loses-6-3b-in-largest-robbery-in-history · 2026-10-30
**Also:** https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/binance-exploit-latest-findings-and-response/

### 2. Bitcoin Core Will Backport Fixes for Easily Discoverable Bugs to All Maintained Releases; 32.1, 31.2, 30.4 and 29.5 Ship Three Fixes (Optech #429)
🔴 Strong · in-force · technical · threat, key-mgmt · [Protocol blog]

**Summary:** Bitcoin Optech #429 reports that Bitcoin Core will backport fixes for vulnerabilities classed as easily discoverable to every maintained release, cut point releases quickly and publish an advisory with them. Releases 32.1, 31.2, 30.4 and 29.5 carry three fixes: Core #36665 (a memory-exhaustion bug on the select() backend used on Windows, macOS and BSD), #36667 (unbounded send-buffer growth) and #36669 (an undo-file overflow). Optech says 32.0 was never released because the fixes were merged after it was tagged. The Bitcoin Core 32.1 release page, dated 2026-10-27, says easily discoverable vulnerabilities will be disclosed once fixed releases exist for all maintained majors, and the security advisories page lists three advisories dated 2026/10/28.
**Filter:** People who validate with their own node depend on the node project's patch cadence, and this changes how quickly a fix reaches every supported version.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/30/ · 2026-10-30
**Also:** https://bitcoincore.org/en/releases/32.1/ · https://bitcoincore.org/en/security-advisories/

### 3. Update: Eclair 0.14.6 Is a Security Release for Splicing and On-the-Fly Funding Issues (Optech #429)
🔴 Strong · in-force · technical · threat, key-mgmt · [Protocol blog]

**Summary:** Optech #429 calls Eclair 0.14.6 a security release for splicing and on-the-fly funding issues. The Eclair release page says "We highly recommend upgrading, as some of these issues can be exploited by malicious nodes", and adds a parameter, off by default, that controls whether splices can spend unconfirmed inputs. This follows the 0.14.4 and 0.14.5 security releases covered the week before.
**Filter:** People who run their own Lightning node hold their channel funds, and a node that has not upgraded stays exposed to counterparties that exploit the fixed issues.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/30/ · 2026-10-30
**Also:** https://github.com/ACINQ/eclair/releases/tag/v0.14.6

## UX & Interaction Signals

### Update: MetaMask Agent Wallet Goes Live as a Grok Bot Plugin, with Guard Mode Limits and Out-of-Band Approval
🔴 Strong · in-force · technical · agentic, key-mgmt, ux · [Protocol blog] · `partial-enforcement-perimeter`

**What changed:** The post says MetaMask Agent Wallet is now a plugin in the Grok Bot marketplace, where Grok Bot can check balances, swap and bridge, trade Hyperliquid perps, take Polymarket positions and supply to yield vaults from plain-language requests. Guard Mode, the recommended setting, restricts activity to allowlisted networks, addresses and recipients and caps outflows over a user-set rolling 24-hour window. Anything outside those bounds pauses until the user approves it through an email link or a MetaMask Mobile push notification. Beast Mode reduces interruptions, but threat scanning stays on and malicious or risky transactions still need approval.
**Experience filter:** The user sets the allowlists and the cap once, then answers approval requests outside the chat where the bot runs. For perps the outflow limit applies to deposits into Hyperliquid, not to individual orders or positions, and Polymarket orders on the off-chain order book are not evaluated one by one.
**Design read (analysis):** The limit is shown as a single cap, but the post lists actions it does not measure, so the user's mental model of "capped" has to include where the cap stops.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://metamask.com/news/metamask-agent-wallet-grok-bot · 2026-10-28

### Safe Policy Engine Announced: Onchain Spending, Recipient and Authority-Change Rules That Signers Cannot Override (Planned Q4 2026)
🟡 Weak · early · technical · ux, key-mgmt, agentic · [Protocol blog] · `delayed-guardrail-loosening`

**What changed:** The post presents the Safe Policy Engine as not yet launched, with a Design Partner Program open on testnet and availability for Safe Pro Business and Enterprise plans planned for Q4 2026. Teams choose rules from guided templates in the Safe Workspace: recipient allowlist, spending limits, app allowlist, authority-change protection and a co-signer requirement. At least one onchain policy is required during setup, and removing or loosening a policy requires a time delay. A transaction that breaks policy cannot execute however many signers approve it. For rules the chain cannot check, a co-signer evaluates offchain and signs or refuses, and proposers and signers see that result before signing.
**Experience filter:** The user decides the limits at setup time and then cannot override them by collecting more signatures, and must wait out a delay to loosen them.
**Design read (analysis):** The decision moves from each transaction to policy design, where a rule set too loose or too tight becomes the main thing to get right.
**Source:** [Protocol blog] https://safe.global/blog/introducing-the-safe-policy-engine · 2026-10-27

## On the radar
- **Core Lightning Self-Referential Channel Announcement Crashed Nodes Until Removed by Hand; Fixed in 26.04.1 (Optech #429)** · 🟠 Emerging · technical · threat: Optech reports that CLN 25.02 through 26.04 did not check that a channel announcement's two node IDs differ, so a self-referential announcement crashed nodes on load and again on restart until an operator removed it manually. The fix is in CLN 26.04.1, and BOLT7 now requires receivers to check the ordering. [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/30/ · 2026-10-30
- **Proposal: Silent Payments for Multiparty (FROST / MuSig2) Wallets with an Aggregated DLEQ Proof (Optech #429)** · 🟡 Weak · technical · key-mgmt: Optech reports Oleksandr Kurbatov's Delving Bitcoin proposal, in which a multiparty sender computes the ECDH share as a weighted sum of the signers' contributions and an aggregated DLEQ proof shows it was derived correctly, in a two-round flow with a coordinator. Kurbatov says no changes are needed to BIP352, BIP374, BIP375, MuSig2 or FROST, and a paper with security proofs is under internal review. [Protocol blog] https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/30/ · 2026-10-30
- **Who Swept My ETH? A Study of EIP-7702 Sweeper Delegations** · 🟡 Weak · academic · key-mgmt, threat: The preprint reports, for the first ten months of EIP-7702 transactions on mainnet, 4,668,209 malicious delegations, 20,443 attack transactions and 508 sweeper contracts, with about 70% of delegations pointing to sweeper contracts and over 600,000 accounts that had not revoked or updated them promptly. It introduces the SweeperHunter detection framework. [arXiv paper] https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41153 · 2026-10-30
- **Credential Pooling Attacks on the EUDI Wallet Architecture and the CRESTA Countermeasure** · 🟡 Weak · academic · identity, key-mgmt: Rufini and Visconti describe colluding users pooling credentials through a proxy that presents them as one synthetic user, tested on the European Commission's reference implementation and on two Android phones. They propose CRESTA, in which an issuer certifies at issuance that a set of keys came from the same secure hardware. [arXiv paper] https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41655 · 2026-10-30
