# Self-Custody & Sovereignty Signal Report — Week of Sep 21–28, 2026

## Top 3 Signals

### 1. Budgeted Threshold Signatures: Protocol-Native Spending Limits with Zero On-Chain Footprint
🔴 Strong · academic · agentic, key-mgmt · [IACR paper]

**Summary:** Researchers formally prove that any naive MPC-comparison approach to budget enforcement has a deterministic bypass: an honest-majority coalition of signers can produce over-budget signatures and remain undetectable to transcript auditors. They introduce BTS, a Schnorr-based threshold construction that embeds per-epoch spending caps as secret-shared state enforced inside the signing session itself, with no on-chain footprint and no dependency on an external policy engine. The paper cites 100M+ agent payments on a single chain in under a year as its motivation.

**Filter:** Changes HOW sovereignty is exercised. Agent spending limits become cryptographically enforced at the key layer rather than the application-policy layer, which removes the operator trust assumption built into every current agentic wallet.

**Source:** [IACR paper] https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2176 · Received Sep 23 · Approved Sep 26, 2026

### 2. Draft EIP PR #12383: Quantum Freeze and Account Recovery
🟠 Emerging · technical · recovery, key-mgmt · [EIP]

**Summary:** A new EIP PR (opened by `colinlyguo`, Sep 26) proposes a minimal Ethereum protocol framework for (a) freezing legacy ECDSA accounts that miss the quantum migration window and (b) defining successor recovery pathways for frozen accounts. It references active Ethereum Magicians discussions on Q-day preparedness and sets the recovery rules at the design stage rather than during a crisis.

**Filter:** Changes WHO holds the keys. Quantum-frozen accounts transfer effective key control from the original ECDSA holder to whoever controls the protocol-defined recovery mechanism, which makes recovery design a first-order sovereignty decision for every Ethereum self-custody wallet.

**Source:** [EIP] https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12383 · Sep 26, 2026 · Draft, pending editor review

### 3. Ledger: Native Shielded Zcash Signing on Secure Element
🟠 Emerging · technical · hardware, key-mgmt · [Vendor security report]

**Summary:** Ledger announced native Sapling/Orchard shielded Zcash transaction signing directly on the secure element. Previously, shielded ZEC spends required a companion hot wallet, which exposed privacy-transaction key material to software. Shielded signing now happens on the device and only signed transactions leave the hardware.

**Filter:** Changes HOW sovereignty is exercised for ZEC privacy transactions. Shielded key material no longer leaves the secure element.

**Source:** [Vendor security report] https://www.ledger.com/blog-native-shielded-zcash-support · Sep 23, 2026

## UX & Interaction Signals

### Safe{Wallet} web-v1.101.0: Proposer Role Education and Spending Limits Pre-Flow
🟠 Emerging · technical · ux, agentic · [GitHub release] · **ux_pattern:** comprehension gate before delegation

**Summary:** Two custody-delegation UX changes shipped together. (1) **Proposer role modal:** an in-product explanation now appears before users can add a proposer address. Previously, users granted transaction-queue rights to a third party with no explanation that the proposer could submit transactions without being a co-signer. (2) **Spending limits pre-flow:** a new explanation screen comes before the spending-limits form, so users understand they are granting unilateral transfer rights up to a ceiling before they set the ceiling. Both changes add comprehension gates at the point of trust delegation where there were none.

**Experience filter:** Changes what a user must understand and decide before granting custody delegation.

**Source:** [GitHub release] https://github.com/safe-global/safe-wallet-monorepo/releases/tag/web-v1.101.0 · Sep 22, 2026
